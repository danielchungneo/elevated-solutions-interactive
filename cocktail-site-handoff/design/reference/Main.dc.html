Margarita

  • Tequila
  • •
  • Citrus
  • •
  • Classic

Bright, tart and salt-kissed.

The Margarita is tequila, lime and orange liqueur in near-perfect balance. It's sharp, a little sweet, and made for a salted rim. Once you've made one properly, you'll never go back to the bottled mix.

Difficulty
Easy
Prep
5 min
Glass
Rocks

A coupe or classic margarita glass also works if you serve it without ice.

Method

Cook mode
  1. 1

    Chill the glass

    Fill your glass with ice and water while you prep. A cold glass keeps the drink crisp for longer.

  2. 2

    Rim the glass

    Dump the ice water. Run a lime wedge around the outer edge of the rim only, then roll the outside of the rim in salt on a plate.

    Tip

    Salt only the outside so it doesn't fall into the drink and make it too salty.

  3. 3

    Add the spirits and juice

    To a shaker, add the tequila, lime juice and orange liqueur. Measure with a jigger. A Margarita depends on those ratios.

  4. 4

    Shake hard

    Fill the shaker about two-thirds with ice and shake hard for 12 to 15 seconds. This chills the drink, adds the right amount of water, and lightens the texture.

  5. 5

    Strain over fresh ice

    Fill the rimmed glass with fresh ice and strain the drink over it. Fresh ice dilutes slowly. The ice you shook with is already broken down.

    Tip

    If you prefer it up, strain into a chilled coupe without ice.

  6. 6

    Garnish and taste

    Add a lime wheel on the rim. Take a sip. If it's too tart or too sweet for you, adjust the next one slightly.

Pro tips

  • Taste your limes. Very tart limes can pull the drink off balance. Small tweaks next round are better than changing the base spec.

  • Keep the salt on the outside edge so each sip is salty and the drink isn't.

Common mistakes

  • Using bottled sour mix. Fresh lime juice is the single biggest upgrade.

  • Shaking for only a few seconds. Under-shaken Margaritas are warm and sharp.

Variations

  • Tommy's Margarita

    Replace the orange liqueur with agave syrup for a cleaner, more tequila-forward drink.

  • Spicy Margarita

    Muddle two or three jalapeño slices in the shaker before adding the other ingredients, or use a chili-salt rim.

  • Smoky Margarita

    Swap a portion of the tequila for mezcal for a smoky edge.

  • Frozen Margarita

    Blend the ingredients with about a cup of ice until slushy and pour into a chilled glass.
